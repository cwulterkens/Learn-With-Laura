# Journey resource design

Laura's Adventure uses a supportive Oregon Trail–style resource loop. Resources create planning choices and visible consequences, but they never create a game-over state or lock a learner out of school content.

| Resource | Gameplay purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Miles | Unlock later adventure chapters. A well-supplied family travels farther at each knowledge stop; low supplies or energy produce a gentler pace. |
| Day | Shows time passing. Every travel stop and camp choice advances the day, making preparation and rest visible parts of the journey. |
| Food | Is used while traveling and by a rest day. Low food slows travel; an empty basket affects health until supplies are gathered. |
| Wood | Is used to keep camp ready. Low wood slows travel; an empty pile affects morale until it is replenished. |
| Health | Represents physical readiness, not academic ability. Low health slows travel and can be restored by resting or taking a movement break. |
| Morale | Represents the family's shared energy. Low morale slows travel and can be restored by rest, calming breaks, music, and family activities. |
| Current region | Changes the chapter context and the balance of food, wood, and morale gained by gathering supplies. |

On the chapter map, the learner can choose **Gather Supplies** or **Rest Together** at any time. Gathering rewards differ by region. Rest uses one food when available and restores health and morale. Family mini-games also award clearly labeled resources, and the recess screen lets the learner choose between a more physical health-focused break and a more calming morale-focused break.

Wrong educational answers never reduce journey resources. Explanations are part of learning, not a punishment. If food, wood, health, or morale reaches zero, automatic family/community support restores a small safe amount before the next travel stop. Chapter access depends on miles, so recovery choices always allow the journey to continue fully offline.
